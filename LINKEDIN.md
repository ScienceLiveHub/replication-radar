## Option A — from Jean Iaquinta (personal · recommended)

A citation used to mean "someone checked this." That's quietly breaking.

For the **OpenAIRE AI Hackathon**, our Science Live team built **Replication Radar** — a tool that makes the OpenAIRE Graph more useful for *replication*.

Search a research field, and it shows two things a citation count can't:
🔎 what's most worth replicating (impact-ranked)
✅ whether a claim has already been **independently checked** — with the verdict, read live from signed nanopublications

If it hasn't been checked, it hands you the independent, reusable software to do it — and the data.

We put it to the test: an AI agent used the Radar (alongside the OpenAIRE MCP) to find an un-replicated Nature paper on marine heatwaves (Oliver et al. 2018). We then ran a genuinely independent replication — different data (ESA CCI satellite SST), different code. It held up: **Validated**.

It's a live web app (no account needed) **and** an MCP server, so any MCP-capable AI agent can use it.

What we care about:
• **Grounded** — every signal from a named public source, nothing guessed
• It makes **research software engineers visible** — the people who build the tools replication runs on
• Built human-led with AI assistance (Claude Code), openly disclosed. Everything CC-BY.

Huge credit to **Anne Fouilloux**, who built it, and **Saranjeet Kaur Bhogal** (Imperial College London), whose RSE insight shaped what we surface — and to **OpenAIRE** for a brilliant hackathon.
